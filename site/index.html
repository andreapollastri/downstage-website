The console

A DESK, NOT A SPREADSHEET

Every strip looks and works like a channel on a large-format console — and every strip has the same sections at the same height, so your eyes read the whole board at once.

01

BIG-DESK STRIPS

High-pass, four fully parametric bands over a live analyser, four inserts, eight FX and eight aux sends, pan, a one-VCA selector and slim console faders with gain-reduction meters.

02

GROUPS A–D

Four stereo groups that are full channel strips. Route any channel with the M and A–D buttons right beside its fader.

03

CUE MIXES THAT WORK

Eight stereo aux outputs, pre or post, with a seven-band EQ, the master into the phones and a click that stays out of the room.

04

EVERYTHING LINES UP

Channels, click, groups, FX returns, VCAs, aux outputs and master, left to right, over the whole screen — or Mix, FX and Rack each on a display of their own. Solo a group or a return and its channels come with it; solo safe and AFL on the auxes, like a real desk. And under the master's EQ, PUMP IT: a one-knob bus compressor, glue to the left, the pump of a club to the right.

Mixing assistant · new in 1.3

A SECOND PAIR OF EARS.

Open the AI tab of the inspector (⌘J) and say what the mix should become. The assistant measures every channel and, if you like, a minute of the mix, reads the console, and answers with a plan of moves — faders, pans, EQ, sends, routing, inserts with their presets, effect buses, clip gain, fades, trims — each with its reason. Look it over and press Apply: the whole plan is one undo step. Discard it and nothing has moved.

Start from a ready-made request — Balance the mix, Like a reference song, More pop, Rougher, in your face, Warm and vintage, Vocal forward, Master this song, Clean up the edit — or write it in your own words: “kick and bass tighter, vocal brighter”, “take the mud out of the guitars”. The next request remembers the last ones, so “a bit less” works. The guide has the details.

IT MEASURES FIRST

Peak, RMS, integrated loudness, crest and loudness range, the tonal balance in eight bands, stereo correlation, how much of the time there is signal and the silence at the edges of every clip — channel by channel, and for the mix.

YOUR MODEL, YOUR KEY

Claude, GPT, Gemini, or any service or local server that speaks the OpenAI API — Mistral, OpenRouter, Ollama, LM Studio and more — with an API key of your own, billed by the service per use: a few cents a request on the big models.

NEVER THE AUDIO

It does nothing until you ask. Then it sends your request, the names and settings of the console and the measurements — never the audio, the recordings or the files. Keys stay on your Mac, in the keychain.

Claude, Anthropic, GPT, OpenAI, Gemini, Google and the other names belong to their owners. There is no affiliation or endorsement. What is sent, and to whom, is in section 2.5 of the Privacy Notice.

Control surfaces

HANDS ON THE FADERS.

Downstage speaks Mackie Control and HUI, the two protocols fader controllers and digital consoles use to drive a DAW, over USB or network MIDI. Motorized faders, V-Pots for pan, sends and EQ, mute, solo, record arm and select on every strip, the transport, markers, the jog wheel, scribble strips, the time display and the meters all follow the mix, with up to two extenders.

Compatible with Behringer X-Touch, X-Touch Extender, Compact, One and Mini, Mackie Control Universal Pro, PreSonus FaderPort 8, 16 and FaderPort, SSL UF8 and UF1, iCON QCon and Platform, Korg nanoKONTROL, keyboards with a DAW mode such as Arturia KeyLab, Novation Launchkey and M-Audio Oxygen Pro — and with the DAW remote layer of Behringer X32 and Midas M32, Behringer WING, Yamaha DM3, DM7, 01V96i, 02R96, DM1000 and DM2000, Allen & Heath SQ, Qu, Avantis and dLive, PreSonus StudioLive Series III, Audient ASP8024-HE and Tascam Model 12. The guide sets each one up, model by model.

FADERS & V-POTS

Touch a fader and the screen follows; the V-Pots pan, or with Send, Plug-in and EQ take the FX sends, aux sends and the four EQ bands of the selected channel. Flip swaps them onto the faders.

TRANSPORT & MARKERS

Play, stop, record and punch, loop, click, a marker at the cursor, F1–F8 to jump to the first eight, save, undo and the automation modes, a jog wheel that scrubs, nudges or zooms.

DISPLAYS & METERS

Names and values on the scribble strips, bars or the clock on the time display, the LEDs for mute, solo, arm and select, the meters of the strips in view, the rude solo light.

ANY VIEW, ANY DESK

Bank through the channels, the groups and FX returns, the aux outputs, the VCAs or the whole console; a console's own eight remote faders work the same way as a dedicated controller.

Recording

HIT RECORD. KEEP EVERY TAKE.

Nothing you play is ever thrown away. Record over what is there and the old pass stays underneath; punch in on the fly or on a range with pre-roll; loop a section for as many passes as it takes. Then drag across the best moment of each take and press “Use this” — comping the way the big DAWs taught your hands, with markers where you expect them and the mix out of the door in WAV or MP3.

NON-DESTRUCTIVE

New passes land on top, drawn as you play; the old ones stay underneath and in the take list, ready to comp, in files named after the channel and the take.

PUNCH & PRE-ROLL

Free punch while playing, auto punch on the loop range, 1–4 bars of pre-roll, loop recording.

COMPING

Open the takes under a track, drag across the good bit of any of them and press “Use this”.

TEMPO, GRID & QUANTIZE

Find the BPM and the grid in the audio — steady, or bar by bar for a band without a click — then quantize the hits, phase-locked across a drum kit. Plus a click strip with its own EQ and headphone sends.

MARKERS

Memory locations: keypad Enter drops one, “. 3 .” jumps there, ⌘5 lists them all with the export range; right click the markers bar to set the loop in and out.

PITCH EDITOR

Detect the notes of a vocal or a bass, drag them to other notes, snap the whole clip to a key and scale, or draw the pitch you want with a pencil right on the waveform. Non-destructive, rendered live. A Pitch Corrector unit does it automatically on a live voice.

ELASTIC AUDIO

⌥-drag a clip's edge and it stretches or shrinks to the new length at the same pitch, from 25% to 400%, without touching the file. Splits, trims, loops and quantize follow it.

CHANNEL NOTES

A post-it under the inserts of every channel — the mic, the take to keep, what is still to do — saved with the session as you type, with a dot on the strip when there is one.

WAV & MP3

The mix, every stem or every output, of the song, the IN–OUT range or the loop, with a real progress bar.

START THE SIZE YOU NEED

A new session starts from an empty console, from a built-in one with 8, 16, 24, 32 or 48 tracks and the inputs in order, or from one of your templates. Any track can be deleted.

STEREO IN ONE SLOT

A stereo channel is one track in the Edit window, marked ST, with both waveforms in its clips and two meters in the header. Click a name to select, ⇧ or ⌘ click for more, double-click to rename.

INPUT MONITOR

I sits next to R on every track: hear what comes into the channel. ⌥ click sets every channel, ⌥⇧ click the selected ones.

Tempo and quantize

TIGHT WHEN YOU WANT IT.

The metronome in the edit bar listens to your session and finds the BPM, the beat and the first downbeat. Steady tempo for music played to a click; Follow the performance for a band that breathes — a tempo for every bar, so the grid follows them.

Then press Q. Every attack is cut a few milliseconds before it and moved onto the grid, the slice before stretches to meet it and a short crossfade joins them. Select the whole kit: every track gets the same cuts and stays in phase.

  • 1/4 to 1/32 · triplets
  • Strength & swing
  • Hits or clip starts
  • Already tight? Left alone
  • One undo

Questions

FAQ

What's new in 1.4?

PUMP IT, a bus compressor built into the master: one knob, a fixed programme, glue to the left and a squashed mix that pumps on the kick to the right (the side chain ignores the sub), with its gain-reduction meter on the strip and in the full master; the mixing assistant can switch it in and set it. A new demo song, “Kreuzberg”: Berlin synth-pop at 118 BPM in 24 channels, every sound synthesised, mixed with groups, VCAs, effects, outboard, amps, PUMP IT, rides, takes and notes. The guide, in six languages, describes all of it with new screenshots.

What's new in 1.3?

A mixing assistant in the AI tab of the inspector (⌘J): it measures every channel and a minute of the mix, reads the console and answers with a plan of moves — faders, pans, EQ, sends, routing, inserts with their presets, effect buses, clip gain, fades, trims — that you look over and apply as one undo step; it runs on Claude, GPT, Gemini or any OpenAI-compatible service or local server, with your own API key. Mackie Control and HUI control surfaces, from an X-Touch or a FaderPort to the DAW layer of an X32 or a DM7. New sessions start empty, or from built-in consoles of 8, 16, 24, 32 or 48 tracks; every track can be deleted; a stereo channel takes one track slot; an input monitor switch (I) next to R on every track; the loop in and out from the markers bar. If you have 1.0, 1.1 or 1.2, the app finds 1.3 by itself and offers to install it.

What's new in 1.2?

Elastic audio: ⌥-drag a clip's edge and it stretches or shrinks at the same pitch, without touching the file. A pitch editor on every clip: detect the notes of a vocal or a bass, drag them, snap the clip to a key and scale, or draw the pitch with a pencil on the waveform; a Pitch Corrector unit does it live. A notes post-it on every channel. The master and every aux patched to any output pair, or to No out. Under the hood, the audio now streams from a disk cache instead of filling the memory, exports stream to disk, the engine follows device and sample-rate changes and restarts itself if it ever stops. If you have 1.0 or 1.1, the app finds 1.2 by itself and offers to install it.

What's new in 1.1?

A pedalboard with 33 classic stompboxes; a mastering section with the transistor mastering chain, Two Stage, Big Passive EQ and Sheen EQ; the Crystal Hall with its remote, the Quad FX and the Echo Chambers; Rig Capture, a tuner and an EQ tuned by note; transistor-desk, broadcast, touring-desk and discrete-console units; over 600 factory presets plus your own; tempo and grid detection and quantize; the waveform while recording; Edit, Mix, FX and Rack on extra displays; session templates; dBFS meters and a master meter in the top bar; a clock-only mode for live recordings. If you have 1.0, it finds 1.1 by itself and offers to install it.

Is it really free?

Yes. Downstage is free for personal and professional use, including recording, mixing and releasing music commercially. You keep every right to your music.

Can I buy you a beer?

Only if you feel like it. Downstage stays free either way. If a song you made with it is out in the world and you want to say thanks, buy me a beer — or a brand-new Telecaster, should the night have gone that well.

Can I share, mirror or sell it?

No. Download it only from downstage.it. Redistributing, re-uploading, bundling, selling, renting or modifying the software is not allowed by the License Agreement.

Does it run on Intel Macs?

This version is built for Apple Silicon Macs (M1 and later) with macOS 13 Ventura or newer.

Does it load plugins?

No. Downstage comes with its own 66 rack units, 33 pedals and 12 effects, built into the engine, so sessions always open the same way on any Mac.

Can it find the tempo of a band recorded without a click?

Yes. Detect tempo listens to the session (or to the tracks you pick) and offers one steady tempo, or a tempo for every bar that follows the performance; it can also move the song so the first downbeat lands on a bar line. Quantize then works on that grid.

Can I use a control surface or my console?

Yes. Downstage speaks Mackie Control (MCU) and HUI over MIDI, so fader controllers such as the Behringer X-Touch, Mackie MCU Pro, PreSonus FaderPort, SSL UF8, iCON QCon and Korg nanoKONTROL work, and so does the DAW remote layer of consoles like the Behringer X32 and Midas M32, WING, Yamaha DM3 and DM7, Allen & Heath SQ, Qu, Avantis and dLive, PreSonus StudioLive Series III and Tascam Model 12. Put the device in its Mackie Control or HUI mode, pick its MIDI ports in the Audio panel, and the faders, sends, mute, solo, transport and displays follow the mix. The guide explains it model by model. Surfaces that only speak EUCON (Avid) are not supported.

What does the AI assistant need, and what does it send?

An API key of your own from Anthropic (Claude), OpenAI (GPT), Google (Gemini) or another service that speaks the OpenAI API — or a model running on your own Mac, with Ollama or LM Studio. The service bills the key per use, a few cents a request on the big models; a chat subscription to ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini cannot be used by another app. The assistant does nothing until you ask; then it sends your request, the names and settings of the console and measurements of the audio made on your Mac — never the audio itself, your recordings or your files. See section 2.5 of the Privacy Notice.

Can I use more than one screen?

Yes. With more displays connected, send Edit, Mix, FX or Rack full screen to each of them; every screen works on the same session.

Are the amps and compressors official?

No. They are independent models inspired by classic gear. Product names are used only to describe the sound they recall and belong to their owners; there is no affiliation or endorsement.

Where does the demo song come from?

It was created by Andrea Pollastri with Claude for Downstage: “Kreuzberg”, an instrumental in the Berlin synth-pop style of the 80s, thirteen synthesised stems at 118 BPM, mixed in Downstage. It comes with the app to learn the console: open it, take it apart and remix it. See section 7 of the License Agreement.

Does it collect data?

The app works offline, with no account and no telemetry; on its own it only asks downstage.it whether a newer version is out (you can turn that off). The mixing assistant, if you set it up, talks to the AI service you chose only when you send it a request. The website uses no cookies. It counts visits with a script from stats.misterdev.it; see the Privacy Notice.

How do updates arrive?

Downstage checks this site for a new version when it starts and from its menu. When one is out it asks, downloads it, checks its signature and checksum, and installs it when you quit — your session is saved first. If you prefer, download the disk image here and drag the app over the old one.
