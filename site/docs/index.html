Downstage

Guide

The same guide as in the app (F1), and the full shortcut list from the ? button.

Getting started

Downstage (DS) is a multitrack recorder and an analog-style mixing console for macOS: up to 48 channels, 8 effect buses, 8 stereo aux outputs, 4 stereo groups (A–D), 8 VCAs, a rack of 64 units and a native audio engine. It has four windows, chosen from the tabs in the top bar or with ⌘1 ⌘2 ⌘3 ⌘4; ⌘= flips between Edit and Mix like Pro Tools.

  • Edit: the timeline. Record, import, cut, move, fade, loop, normalize and comp your takes; every track has a small level meter.
  • Mix: the console, left to right: channels, the click, groups, FX returns, VCAs, aux outputs and the master, over the whole height of the window.
  • FX: the eight effect units fed by the FX sends.
  • Rack: 64 slots for any unit — dynamics, channel strips, amps, pedals, EQs, tape, the tuner — patched onto any strip.

The top bar holds the transport (return to start, stop, play, record), the time display — click it to switch between bars and tempo and a clock only in hours, minutes, seconds and milliseconds, for live recordings — tempo and meter at the cursor, Loop, Punch, Pre-roll and Click. On the right: a small master meter with its peak (click the number to clear it), the audio device, the inspector, the screens button (with more displays), the sessions button (the folder), undo, save, the shortcuts (?) and this guide (F1). Drag the top bar to move the window; in full screen it steps down a little, so reaching for it does not bring down the macOS menu bar.

First steps: choose your audio interface (next chapter), import WAV files with ⌘I or drag them from the Finder, arm a track and record, then open the Mix window.

Sessions screen: when Downstage opens, and every time you click the folder in the top bar, it offers New session (from the empty console or one of your templates), Open session, your Recent sessions, your session templates and the Demo session — an instrumental song made for Downstage: 22 channels already mixed with groups, VCAs, effects, outboard, automation and markers, made to be taken apart. Esc, or “Back to …”, returns to the session you were in. Before a session is replaced Downstage always asks, and offers to save it when it has changes; quitting asks too.

More screens: with more displays connected, the screens button sends Edit, Mix, FX or Rack — any page not already showing — full screen to another display, with no top bar. Every screen works on the same session: a change on one shows on the others at once, and a dot on the tab marks a page that is on another screen. The × in its corner closes it, and the page is free to go to another display.

Audio setup

Click the device button in the top bar (or the Audio tab of the inspector). Pick your interface as Output: if it also has inputs, it is picked as Input too. On a Mac the built-in microphone and speakers are two devices, so choose both. The Buffer sets latency: 128 or 256 samples while recording, 512 or 1024 while mixing.

The choice is applied at once and remembered: next time Downstage reopens the same interface, and finds it again by itself if you unplug and replug it. Internal runs the session without hardware (silent, no recording).

  • Inputs: each strip has its input selector; the grid in the Audio panel assigns many tracks at once (drag down a column, or Assign 1:1).
  • Outputs: a channel plays in the main mix (outputs 1-2), in its groups, or straight on a physical output of its own (a pair for stereo channels) with the Out selector.
  • Aux outputs: eight sends for headphones and wedges, each stereo on a pair or mono on one output, pre or post fader. ⌥ click a switch to set them all.
  • Groups play into the main mix or on a physical pair of their own.

Up to 64 inputs and outputs are used, so large interfaces work as they are.

Editing

Each track has a header with Mute, Solo, Record arm, phase (Ø), Automation (A) and Takes (T). Drag the coloured square to reorder tracks. + Mono and + Stereo add tracks, up to 48; a stereo pair is two tracks linked as one channel, marked L and R.

  • Slip / Grid (F2 / F4): free moves or moves that snap to the grid; − and = change the grid size. The grid follows the tempo map.
  • Tools: select (F8), trim (F6), split (B).
  • Clips: click to select, drag to move, drag up or down to move to another track, hold ⌥ to drag a copy. Drag the edges to trim. A selected clip shows its fade handles.
  • At the cursor: ⌘E splits, A/S trim start and end, D/G fade in and out, ⌘F crossfades two clips that touch.
  • Zoom: R and T or ⌘[ ⌘], ⌘ + wheel, Fit the song. The strip at the bottom is an overview you can drag.
  • Automation: open A on a track, click to add points, drag them; Read plays them, Write records the fader and pan while playing.

Clips are stacked: the newest one sits on top and hides what is underneath without deleting it. Delete the top clip and the older audio is there again.

Stereo channels, colours, meters

A stereo channel is edited as one: its two tracks are drawn as one block, a bar joining L and R. Selecting, moving, trimming, fading, cutting, deleting, looping or normalizing a clip on one side always does the same on the other; clips move only from a left side to a left side and from right to right; comping a take uses the take recorded on the other side in the same pass.

Colours: click the coloured square of a track (or of a strip in the Mix) for the palette of 24 colours. With several channels selected and the clicked one among them, the colour goes to all of them — or Spread gives each the next colour. Drag the square to reorder.

Every track header has a small level meter. Follow, in the edit bar, keeps the cursor in view while playing and recording: off, a page at a time, or centred. Deleting a channel always asks first; undo brings it back.

Clips: duplicate, loop, normalize, gain, quantize

Right-click a clip, or click ▾ on a selected clip, for its commands. They act on every selected clip, and the two sides of a stereo pair always move together.

  • Duplicate (⌘D): a copy right after the selection.
  • Loop…: repeats the selection N times, one copy after another. Several clips repeat as a block and keep their spacing.
  • Normalize: brings the loudest peak of each clip to 0 dBFS; Clip gain… offers −0.3, −1, −3, −6 and −12 dBFS too. A stereo clip keeps one gain for both sides.
  • Clip gain: from −40 to +20 dB in 1 dB steps, with the slider, the −6…+6 buttons, ⌃⇧↑ / ⌃⇧↓ or the scroll wheel over the dB badge on the clip. The waveform follows the gain and turns red where the peaks would pass full scale.
  • Move to the cursor, Split at the cursor and Delete.

Clip gain acts before the channel, so inserts, compressors and EQ react to the new level.

Quantize

Select clips (or tracks) and press Q, or choose Quantize… from a clip's menu.

  • Hits finds every attack in the selected clips and cuts a few milliseconds before it; each slice moves onto the grid, the slice before it stretches or shortens to meet it and a short crossfade joins them. Hits already within a few milliseconds of the grid are left alone.
  • Select all the tracks of a drum kit together: the attacks are found on all of them at once and every track gets the same cuts and moves, so the kit stays in phase.
  • Clip starts moves each clip so its start falls on the grid — for one-shots, samples and loops.
  • Grid from 1/4 to 1/32 with triplets, Strength (how far toward the grid), Swing and Sensitivity (how soft an attack still counts). One undo puts it back.

Recording, takes and comping

Arm the tracks with R (drag across several R buttons, or ⌥R for all), make sure each one has an input, then press Record (⌘Space, F12 or 3 on the keypad).

  • Pre-roll: playback starts 1, 2 or 4 bars before the cursor and recording begins at the cursor.
  • Free punch: while playing, Record punches in and pressing it again punches out; playback keeps going.
  • Auto punch: drag on the bar ruler to set a range and turn on Punch. Recording starts and stops exactly at the range (shown in red). With Loop on as well, every pass round the loop becomes a take.
  • Click: C or the metronome button. Its strip, right after the channels, sets sound, output, bass and treble, the eight aux sends for the headphones, pan and whether it plays only while recording.

Non-destructive: nothing is ever overwritten. A new recording lands on top; the old audio stays underneath and every pass is kept in the take list.

Comping: click T on a track to open its takes. Drag across the good part of any take and press ↑ Use this (or ⌥⇧↑): that slice is copied on top of the track with short crossfades. Click a take's name to use the whole take, rename it or remove it from the list.

While recording, the red clip of each armed track draws the waveform of what is coming in.

Recorded files are named after the channel and the take, so they are easy to find in the session's audio folder: 03 Kick In - Take 01.wav, and for the two sides of a stereo channel 05-06 Overheads L - Take 02.wav. A number already on disk is never reused, so nothing is ever written over; the take in the list carries the same number.

Tempo, meter and click

The BPM field and the meter menu in the top bar show and change the tempo and meter at the cursor. The thin Tempo ruler above the bars lists every change.

  • Click empty space on the tempo ruler to add a change at that bar; click a marker to edit it or delete it.
  • Meters: 4/4, 3/4, 2/4, 6/8, 5/4, 7/8, 9/8, 12/8, 2/2, 7/4, 5/8. In compound meters the beat is the eighth note.
  • Bars, the grid, snapping, the click, keypad 1/2 to jump by bar and the tempo-synced delays all follow the map.

The click accents the first beat of every bar and follows the meter in force. Choose Beep, Wood, Cowbell, Hi-hat or Rim on its strip.

Markers and the export range

The Markers ruler, between Tempo and Bars, holds the song's sections, as the memory locations of Pro Tools:

  • Enter on the keypad (fn Return or M on a laptop) drops a marker at the cursor, also while playing; double-click the ruler to add one there.
  • . number . jumps to that marker (. 3 . is the third). Click a marker to go there, drag it to move it, double-click to rename it, ⌥ click to delete it.
  • ⌘5 opens Memory Locations: every marker with its bar and time, to rename, recall or delete, and the export range.
  • The export range (blue IN and OUT flags): while playing, ↓ sets the start and ↑ the end, as in Pro Tools; drag the flags, or right-click the ruler. Export uses it when it is set.

Detect tempo

The metronome button in the edit bar listens to the session — or only to the tracks of the selected clips, a drum kit for instance — and finds the tempo, the beat and the first downbeat. Steady tempo is for music played to a click or programmed: one tempo for the whole session, and with Align the song moves (clips, takes, automation and markers together, never before zero) so the first downbeat falls on a bar line. Follow the performance is for a band recorded without a click: a tempo for every bar, so the bars and the grid follow the music as it breathes. The BPM can be corrected and the meter changed before applying; one undo puts everything back.

Clock only

Click the time display in the top bar to put bars and tempo away — for a concert recorded in multitrack — and get a big clock in hours, minutes, seconds and milliseconds; the rulers then count minutes and seconds. The choice is kept with the session; click again to go back to bars.

The Mix window

On the Mix, FX and Rack pages the song bar under the top bar shows the whole song with its markers, the export range and the cursor: click or drag it to move, click a marker to jump to it, right-click it for markers and the export range.

The console takes the whole height of the window. Every strip has the same layout, top to bottom (a stereo link is one strip with two meters):

  • Number and ⤢, which opens the full channel.
  • In, phase Ø, link with the next channel, Out (main or a physical output). Downstage asks before making two channels one stereo channel and before splitting one; split channels start again from the centre.
  • EQ: high-pass and four fully parametric bands coloured like an SSL; each row reads gain, frequency and Q. LF and HF switch between shelf and bell. The real-time analyser runs behind the curve.
  • Inserts 1 to 4 (eight on the master): click an empty one to mount a unit, a full one to edit it, ⌥ click to bypass.
  • FX 1–8 and AUX 1–8: all sixteen sends, two rows of four each.
  • Pan and the VCA selector: like on an SSL, a strip sits on one VCA or none. Use ▲▼ or the scroll wheel, click the number for the list, ⌥ click to clear. On a selected channel it changes every selected channel.
  • M S R, the fader with its slim SSL cap, the level meters with their dB scale, the amber gain-reduction meter of its compressors and, beside the fader, the routing buttons: M (main mix) and A B C D (the groups). Turn M off to hear a channel only through its groups.
  • The name on tape: double-click to rename. The coloured square beside it opens the colour palette.

After the channels comes the narrow click strip, then the groups, the FX returns, the VCAs, the aux outputs and the master. FX returns are channel strips without FX sends and phase that play straight into the main mix and can sit on a VCA; groups are complete channel strips; aux outputs and the master share the output strip with the seven-band EQ. Every strip uses the same sections at the same height, so the rows line up across the whole console.

⌥ click a button for every channel, ⌥⇧ for the selected ones, ⌘ click Solo for an exclusive solo. Right-click a strip to copy and paste its settings or load an EQ preset.

Meters: every level meter reads sample peaks in dBFS, 0 at the top and more room near the top, with ticks at −6, −12, −18, −30 and −50 numbered beside them; the fader keeps its own scale. A mono channel shows its level after the fader and before the pan, so a full-scale track reads 0 dBFS. The bar rises at once and falls back at 20 dB a second. Peak hold: every meter keeps the highest level of the last three seconds as a line, red from 0 dBFS; the number beside the fader value shows the same peak in dB (amber close to 0, red at 0 and over). Click it to clear it. The master's meter is also in the top bar.

Groups, outputs and the click

  • Groups A–D: four stereo groups. Channels feed them with the A–D buttons beside the fader. Each group is a full channel strip: phase, output (main mix or a physical pair), four-band EQ, four inserts, eight FX sends, eight aux sends, balance, VCA and mute. Rename them by double-clicking the tape: drum bus, guitars, vocals, stems for another machine.
  • VCA: a channel, an FX return or a group sits on one VCA. A group on a VCA follows its fader and mute; soloing the VCA solos the channels that feed the group.
  • Solo: every channel, group and FX return has S. Soloing a channel keeps its path open: its groups and the returns it feeds (its reverb stays). Soloing a group lights the channels routed to it; soloing an FX return lights the channels that send to it — their S buttons get an outline. Right-click (or ⌃ click) an S for solo safe, as in Pro Tools: the strip is never muted by another solo (a dot marks it). On an aux the button is AFL: the main outputs play that aux mix, to hear exactly what a musician has in the phones. Solos never change the aux mixes.
  • Aux outputs: eight outputs, stereo on a pair or mono, pre or post fader, with a seven-band EQ and four inserts each.
  • Output EQ: aux outputs and the master have seven bands. The strip shows seven gain sliders from 60 Hz to 12 kHz; ⤢ opens the full view with frequency, Q and shelves, over the live analyser.
  • Master into the auxes: the eight AUX knobs on the master put the whole mix in the headphones, before or after the master fader as each aux is set.
  • Click: a narrow strip after the channels with on/off, sound, output, Rec only, bass and treble (gain and frequency), all eight aux sends taken before its fader (the click in the phones and not in the room) and pan.

The full channel

Press ⤢ on any strip (or right-click → Open channel): channels, FX returns, groups, aux outputs and the master open on one card with everything in view, nothing to scroll. ‹ and › (or the arrow keys) step through them; the name can be changed at the top, and beside it are M and S — R on channels, AFL on an aux — to mute or solo without leaving the window.

  • EQ: the curve over the live spectrum. Drag the numbered points to set frequency and gain, scroll over a point to change its Q, double-click to flatten it; each band has gain, frequency and Q underneath. Presets ▾ holds dozens of starting points by instrument — kick, snare, toms, hi-hat, overheads, room, bass, electric and acoustic guitar, piano, organ, pads, vocals, strings, brass, utilities — and your own.
  • Inserts: the four insert points with their meters; open any unit from here.
  • Input and output, the eight FX and eight aux sends, routing (MAIN, A–D), pan, fader and VCA.

Copy… chooses what to copy: EQ, inserts, FX sends, aux sends, fader and pan, VCA, output and groups, automation, input. Paste applies it here; from a strip's menu or with ⌘⌥V you paste onto every selected channel. ⌘⌥C copies everything from the selected channel.

Inserts and the rack

Every channel, FX return, group, aux output and the master has four insert points, processed in order 1 → 4. The units live in one rack of 64 slots, numbered 1 to 64: any unit fits in any slot. On a stereo channel a unit works in stereo with a linked detector, in a single slot.

  • Mount from a strip (click an empty insert) or in the Rack window: choose the type, the destination and the insert point.
  • A strip of masking tape on each unit shows, in marker, where it is patched.
  • Drag a unit by its handle onto another slot to move or swap it.
  • Every unit has a meter: a needle VU on the vintage ones, LEDs on the modern ones. Dynamics and channel strips with a compressor open on gain reduction; amps, the pedalboard, tape and the 1073 preamp on their input (how hard you drive them); EQs, modulation and the rest on their output. Click a meter to switch between gain reduction (where there is one), input and output.
  • Clicking an insert on a strip opens just that unit in its own window.

Click an empty insert for the full menu: every unit, grouped by kind — dynamics, mastering, channel strips, guitar amps, bass amps, pedals and modulation, EQ and colour, tools. The menus in the Rack and FX windows are grouped the same way.

Faceplates and presets

Every unit is laid out like the hardware, in named blocks — preamp, gate, dynamics, the EQ with a column per band, output — that stay whole when the window is narrow. The switch in use is lit.

Presets ▾ on every unit and every effect lists the factory presets — up to ten per machine, chosen for what it does best: the dbx 160 on kick and bass, the 1176 on vocals and snare, the Pultec trick on the kick… — and your presets. Name the current settings and press Save to keep them: they are stored in Documents/Downstage/Presets and every session has them. Your presets can be overwritten (Save here) and deleted, always after asking; factory presets cannot be changed or deleted.

Dynamics, mastering, channel strips and processors

In the menus, Dynamics holds the compressors, gates and dynamic processors, Mastering the mix-bus and mastering tools (some are described here) and Channel strips the console channels.

  • API 2500: punchy and thick; threshold, ratio, attack, release, knee, Thrust, colour and mix.
  • 1176 FET: very fast; Input drives, Output sets the level; ratios 4, 8, 12, 20 and All buttons.
  • LA-2A: opto leveler with its slow two-stage release; Compress or Limit.
  • Fairchild 670: variable-mu tubes, six time constants.
  • Distressor: ratios from 1:1 (colour only) to Nuke, detector HP and band, Dist 2 and Dist 3 harmonics, Opto and Brit modes.
  • SSL Bus and Manley Vari-Mu: glue for the mix bus and the groups.
  • dbx 160: VCA compression with an RMS detector, an attack that tightens with the transient, a fast constant-rate release and the OverEasy soft knee; made for kick, snare and bass.
  • dbx 166: gate, compressor (OverEasy, Auto timing) and the PeakStop+ limiter in one unit.
  • Drawmer DS201 gate: key high-pass and low-pass filters, threshold, range, attack from 10 µs, hold, decay and Key listen.
  • Multiband compressor: four modules side by side, one per band, each with its own VU meter, threshold, ratio, attack, release and gain; phase-coherent crossovers sum flat when nothing is compressed.
  • De-esser (split or wide, Listen) and Transient designer (attack and sustain).
  • Brickwall limiter: the last unit on the master. A 1.5 ms look-ahead brings the gain down before a peak arrives, so nothing passes the ceiling and nothing clips; Input drives the mix into it, Release sets how smoothly it lets go.

Channel strips:

  • SSL 4000 E: 18 dB/oct high-pass and low-pass, gate/expander, the VCA channel compressor and the four-band EQ with bell switches.
  • Neve 88RS: transformer-coupled preamp, high-pass, four-band EQ and a smooth channel compressor.
  • API: the 512 preamp, the 550A three-band EQ with stepped frequencies and proportional Q, and the 527 compressor with Thrust.
  • Soundcraft and Mackie 24·8: preamp, high-pass, two shelves and two sweepable mids, from warm British to clean.
  • The generic Channel strip: filters, gate, compressor, four-band EQ and drive.

EQ and colour: Pultec EQP-1A, Neve 1073, the tape machine and the 31-band graphic EQ: thirty-one sliders a third of an octave apart from 20 Hz to 20 kHz (±12 dB), a high-pass and a low-pass; it is matched, so the curve passes through every slider and neighbouring bands do not pile up; drag across the sliders to draw the curve, ⌥ click resets one, Flat resets them all. On a mono channel it runs one curve; on a stereo strip Link runs both sides on one curve and Dual gives each side its own (L and R choose which you see) — the classic graphic on a monitor send. Tools: the tuner, the meters and the signal generator for line-up and routing checks.

Meters (in Tools): units that pass the sound untouched and show what it is made of. The Spectrum analyzer reads 1/6 octave from 20 Hz to 20 kHz with peak hold (off, 1 s, 3 s, ∞) and names the loudest frequency; point at the display to read any frequency, and switch to Mid / Side to see the side as a line. The Phase scope is the goniometer: mono is a vertical line, a wide mix a ball, out-of-phase material lies down; the correlation meter underneath reads +1 to −1. The Stereo image shows each octave's width and balance, red where it is out of phase. Besides inserts 1–4 they can sit at a strip's Post point, after its inserts, without taking a slot: the demo has all three at the master's post point.

New units and calibration

The compressors are calibrated for tracks around −18 dBFS RMS: with their default settings they take 1 to 6 dB off, and their colour stays clean at normal levels. On the LA-2A, the Manley and the API 525, Limit is a different animal from Compress — a far higher ratio with a hard knee that grabs sooner.

The menus are grouped by what the units are for. Mastering gathers the mix-bus and mastering tools: the Abbey Road TG Mastering Chain (the EMI TG12410 transfer console — input drive, filters, a three-band EQ with stepped frequencies, the compressor/limiter before or after the EQ, the spreader and the output), the SSL Bus, the Manley Vari-Mu, the Fairchild 670, the Shadow Hills Mastering Compressor (optical and discrete stages, Nickel, Iron or Steel transformer), the multiband, the Manley Massive Passive, the Maag EQ4 with its Air Band, Mid/Side and width (mono lows, side air), the Behringer Edison EX1 stereo enhancer and the brickwall limiter.

  • Dynamics also has the API 525 (the 60s feedback compressor), the Golden Age Comp-54 (after the Neve 2254: diode bridge, ratios 1.5 to 6:1, auto recovery, a limiter), the Alesis 3630 (compressor/limiter with gate: RMS and a fast release on the drum bus is the French house pump), the Behringer Composer (MDX: gate, Interactive Knee, Low Contour, Dynamic Enhancer, peak limiter) and the Midas PRO dynamics (Corrective, Adaptive or Vintage, with a gate).
  • Channel strips: the EMI TG12345 (the Abbey Road desk of the late 60s: the input you can push, bass, treble and the stepped presence, the channel compressor/limiter), the Siemens Sitral channel (V676 preamp, W295b EQ, the console's limiter) and the Midas XL4 channel (the touring console's sweet four-band EQ with sweepable mids and variable width).
  • EQ and colour: the Siemens W295 / W295b (inductor broadcast EQ: Tiefen, Präsenz at 0.7–5.6 kHz, Höhen, rumble and top cut), the API 560 (ten octave bands with proportional Q), the guitar / bass EQ tuned by note — choose the instrument, the key and the scale and each band offers the notes of that scale (body, mud, honk, bite and fizz on a guitar; sub, punch, mud, growl and click on a bass), Hz mode shows the nearest note, Octave lifts the octave above too — and the Lo-fi sampler (SP-1200 and SP-12 rates, bits, drive and the output filter).
  • Tools: the tuner — chromatic, or naming the strings of a guitar or bass tuning (standard, Eb, drop D, DADGAD, open G and D, 4- and 5-string bass), with the note, the cents on a needle or as strobe bands, the frequency and the reference A from 430 to 450 Hz; Mute silences the strip while you tune, like a pedal tuner — the meters described below and the signal generator.

Amps, pedalboard and modulation

Mount an amp on the insert of a DI guitar or bass channel. Every amp has its real channels and controls, a Cabinet switch (or DI) and an Output level; bass amps also have Blend to mix the DI back in.

  • Marshall JCM900 + 1960: channel A clean to crunch, channel B the lead with its diode clipping; gain, volume and reverb per channel, presence, bass, middle, treble, master; 4x12 with Greenbacks.
  • Vox AC30: Normal or Top Boost, volume into the EL84s, treble, bass, Cut, Brilliant; Alnico Blue 2x12.
  • Fender Twin Reverb: Normal or Vibrato channel, bright, scooped tone stack, spring reverb, speed and intensity.
  • Roland JC-120 Jazz Chorus: clean solid state; the effect channel's stereo chorus (dry left, swept right) or vibrato, distortion and reverb.
  • Orange Rockerverb (clean and dirty) and Brunetti (clean, crunch, lead) for modern high gain.
  • Ampeg SVT + 8x10: five-position midrange, Ultra Lo, Ultra Hi. Ashdown ABM: valve drive, sub, mid sweep, Shape, 4x10 or 1x15. Markbass: VLE, VPF and four bands.
  • Multi-effect board (GT style): noise suppressor, compressor, overdrive/distortion (OD-1, Blues, Screamer, DS-1, Metal Zone, Fuzz), EQ, modulation (chorus, flanger, phaser, tremolo, vibrato), digital or tape delay and reverb, each with its switch. Patch loads ready settings: Clean Chorus, Crunch, Blues Lead, Rock Lead, Metal, Ambient Swell, Funk, Shoegaze.
  • Roland Dimension D: the classic "space" box, buttons 1–4 and the 1+4 and 2+4 combinations; wide and mono compatible.
  • Leslie 122: chorale, tremolo and brake with the real spin-up of horn and drum, drive, balance and width.

Pedalboard

Six slots in a row, the order of the chain. Any slot takes any pedal, chosen from a menu grouped by maker, with the controls printed on it and its footswitch (the red LED is lit when it is on); the arrows move a pedal along the chain, and choosing a pedal sets its controls where they sit on a new one.

  • Boss: the drives — DS-1, SD-1, BD-2, OD-1, MT-2; dynamics and EQ — the CS-3 Compression Sustainer, the CP-1X and the GE-7 graphic EQ with its seven sliders; modulation — CE-1, CH-1 Super Chorus, BF-3, PH-3; delay and reverb — DD-3, RE-2, RV-6; the OC-5 octave (Poly for chords, Original for the single notes of the OC-2) and the SY-1 synthesizer (Lead, Pad, Strings, Bell, Organ, Sweep, Pulse and Bass, shaped by Tone/Rate, Depth and Variation); for bass, the BB-1X and ODB-3 drives with the clean bass blended in, the BC-1X compressor, the LMB-3 limiter enhancer, the GEB-7 graphic EQ and the CEB-3 chorus, whose Low Filter keeps the bottom dry and centred.
  • Electro-Harmonix: the Big Muff π (Volume, Tone, Sustain), the Small Stone phaser (Rate and Color) and the Deluxe Memory Man (a bucket-brigade delay with chorus or vibrato on the repeats; Level drives its input).
  • MXR Dyna Comp (Output, Sensitivity) and the Ibanez TS808 Tube Screamer (Overdrive, Tone, Level: the mids are clipped, the bass passes clean).
  • TC Helicon Talkbox Synth: your guitar — fuzzed as through the tube, or turned into a synth or an octave voice — through a mouth that moves between U, O, A, E and I. With no microphone on an insert, the mouth is the Vowel knob, opened further by how hard you play (Talk); Gender moves the formants.
  • Eventide Space: Hall, Room, Plate, Spring, ModEchoVerb, BlackHole, Shimmer and TremoloVerb, with Mix, Decay, Size, Delay, Lo, Hi and Mod. Mr. Black Eterna: a shimmer reverb an octave, a fifth or two octaves up, or an octave down.
  • Roland RE-201 Space Echo (the twelve modes) and RE-555 Chorus Echo (the heads in combination, spring reverb and chorus): the tape echo in the chain, the dry signal kept.
  • Kemper Profiler: twelve profiled rigs, from a black-panel clean to a 5150 and an SVT, each with the cabinet it was profiled through and balanced in level; the Stack section — Gain, Definition, Clarity, Power sagging, Pick and the Compressor — the four-band EQ, the cabinet (or direct) and its Character.
  • Wah (Cry Baby style): the pedal, Auto — the envelope filter of funk and bass, opened by your playing — or an LFO; classic, bass or wide range and Q.
  • Flanger (M117R style): Manual, Width, Speed, Regen (Negative for the hollow sound) and stereo; Mix 50% is the jet, 100% a vibrato.
  • Phaser (Phase 90 style): speed, depth, 4 to 12 stages, the Block or Script voice.

Effects

The FX window holds eight effect units, one per FX bus. A new session starts with this chain: 1 Lexicon 480L, 2 Lexicon Vocal Plate, 3 Delay, 4 Space Echo, 5 Quadraverb, 6 SPX, 7 Chorus, 8 Shimmer. Channels and groups reach them with their FX sends; each return comes back on a channel strip with EQ, four inserts, eight aux sends and a VCA, and plays straight into the main mix. Choose another effect from the menu, or Empty; Presets ▾ on each unit holds its factory presets and yours.

The buses are stereo: a stereo channel keeps its image, and the reverbs take left and right separately. An effect nobody sends to goes to sleep after its tail and costs nothing.

  • Lexicon 480L, with its LARC remote: the red display, twelve program keys (halls, Random Hall, plates, rooms, ambience, chamber) and six faders — RT mid, size, pre-delay, bass multiplier, treble decay and diffusion — plus Spin and Wander. Loading a program sets the faders, as on the LARC; ⌥ click a fader for the program's value.
  • Hall and Plate: a large room with early reflections, and a dense, bright plate.
  • Delay: tempo-synced, darker on every repeat, ping-pong.
  • Chorus: two swept voices. Flanger: the jet on a send, wet only, so the channel and the return make the comb.
  • Space Echo: the Roland RE-201 with three heads, the twelve mode positions, repeat rate, intensity (it can self-oscillate), bass, treble, wow and saturation.
  • Shimmer: an Eno/Lanois style hall whose repeats climb an octave or a fifth; decay up to 30 s.
  • Abbey Road Chambers: the echo chambers under Studio Two — the speaker and the microphones in a tiled room, mic distance from the first reflections to the tail, warm or bright speaker — fed, if you like, through STEED, the tape delay with feedback that went before the chamber.
  • Quadraverb: the Alesis chain — EQ, chorus, flanger or detune, delay and reverb in series — in sixteen programs: chorus hall, gated and reverse reverbs, flange delay, multi-tap, detune doubler and all four at once; 16-bit for its early converters.
  • Lexicon: halls, plates, chambers, rooms, ambience and the chorus/echo combinations; rev time, spin, bass multiply, treble.
  • SPX: the classic Yamaha programs, from reverbs and gates to flanger, symphonic and pitch change; Vintage makes it an SPX90.

Sessions, import and export

Each session is a folder in Documents/Downstage with project.json and its audio in audio/. Name it in the top bar; the folder follows.

  • New ⌘N asks for a name and what to start from: the Empty console (always there, it cannot be deleted) or one of your templates. Open ⌘O, Undo ⌘Z, redo ⌘⇧Z. The folder in the top bar opens the sessions screen.
  • Save: the save button offers Save session (audio + mix) — also ⌘S — and Save as template (mix only): the console without the song — channels with their names, colours, inputs, EQ, sends and routing, the rack, the effects, groups, auxes, VCAs, the click and the master — without clips, takes, automation and markers. Templates live in Documents/Downstage/Templates; one with the same name is replaced only after asking; delete them from the sessions screen.
  • Leaving a session (new, open, demo, recent) and quitting Downstage always ask first; with changes not saved you can save, or leave without saving.
  • Import ⌘I or drag WAV files and folders from the Finder: each file gets a track and is copied into the session.
  • Recordings are named after the channel and the take: 03 Kick - Take 01.wav.
  • Export (File menu or the Files tab) asks what and how: the stereo mix, one stem per channel (through its groups, as you hear it) or all the outputs in one multichannel file; the whole song, the export range or the loop; WAV at the session rate or 44.1–96 kHz, 16-bit (dithered), 24-bit or 32-bit float, or MP3 at 128–320 kbps, 44.1 or 48 kHz (encoded with LAME); and how much reverb tail to keep. A progress bar shows the real state of the render and of the MP3 encoding, with Cancel, and Show in Finder at the end.
  • Your presets are kept in Documents/Downstage/Presets and every session has them.
  • Sessions from earlier versions open and are converted automatically.
